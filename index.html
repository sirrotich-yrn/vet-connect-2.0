Farm notes

Livestock knowledge worth keeping in mind

Short, practical notes drawn from Kenyan veterinary and agricultural sources — a rotating reminder while you're here.

💉

Vaccine schedules aren't one-size-fits-all

Foot-and-mouth disease protection in cattle only lasts around six months, so it needs a booster roughly twice a year — while a single dose against PPR can protect sheep and goats for life.

Kenya Veterinary Vaccines Production Institute
🗺

Vaccination priorities differ by region

National campaigns tend to target foot-and-mouth disease in dairy areas and PPR in arid and semi-arid zones, since an outbreak can trigger quarantines that stop a farmer from selling animals at all.

Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development
🐓

Village poultry can skip the cold chain

A heat-stable strain of Newcastle disease vaccine lets free-range chickens be protected from about a week old, with boosters roughly every three months, without needing refrigeration in the field.

AfroTools agricultural data
🪱

Deworm before you vaccinate

An animal carrying a heavy parasite load mounts a weaker immune response to a vaccine, so leaving one to two weeks between deworming and vaccination gets more out of both.

Agriculture.Institute best-practice notes
🌾

Dry-season feed shortages are predictable

Smallholder herds that rely on Napier grass and crop residue tend to run short around September and October — farmers who bank surplus feed as hay or silage in the wet season ride that gap out more easily.

Smallholder dairy feeding research, semi-arid Kenya
🌿

Fodder trees can rival concentrate feed

Calliandra, a fodder shrub grown across East Africa, carries protein levels close to commercial dairy concentrate and has been linked to a real lift in milk income where farmers plant enough of it.

Dairy nutrition research, Nyeri and Meru counties
🦟

Time skin-disease vaccines around the rains

Cattle are usually first vaccinated against Lumpy Skin Disease around six months old, with a yearly booster best given just before the rains, when biting insects that spread the virus are most active.

Livestock vaccine guidance, Kenyan farm advisories
🌡

The cold chain is where programs quietly fail

Most vaccines need to stay between about 2°C and 8°C from purchase to injection — a gap in that chain is one of the most common, least visible reasons a vaccination doesn't take.

Livestock vaccination guidance, Kenyan farm advisories
